Statistical Analysis Plan

Overview

This document is the integrated CO-LUMINATE Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP) for Objectives 2-5. It brings together the finalized psychometric, trajectory, pathway-screening, and causal mediation specifications used across the current project workflows, while preserving the public-facing section structure.

The current release includes:

  • Section A: Objective 2 (Depression Harmonization)
  • Section B: Objective 3 (Social Determinant Trajectories)
  • Section C: Objective 4 (Behavioral Mediators)
  • Section D: Objective 5 (Causal Mediation)

Section A: Objective 2 - Depression Harmonization SAP

A1 Background and Rationale

This section defines the psychometric protocol for harmonizing depression measurement between the Shona Symptom Questionnaire and the Patient Health Questionnaire-9. The central challenge is not simple score conversion, but defensible alignment of instruments that differ in item format, recall period, and cultural grounding in a longitudinal multi-cohort setting.

The PHQ-9 is treated as the external benchmark for probable depression (Kroenke et al. 2001), while the SSQ contributes culturally grounded distress content that is relevant in Southern African settings (Patel et al. 1997; Haney et al. 2014; Chibanda et al. 2019). Objective 2 therefore treats harmonization as a staged psychometric program: item-set refinement, structural validation, invariance assessment, joint IRT calibration, and threshold comparison. In the finalized implementation, the theoretically derived SSQ-10 subset is retained as the main transportable SSQ configuration because it preserves the depressive core while avoiding non-specific distress items that weaken comparability with the PHQ benchmark (Putnick and Bornstein 2016; Woods 2009).

A3 Objectives and Estimands

Let \(Y_i^{PHQ} = \mathbb{1}\{PHQ9_i \ge 10\}\) denote the PHQ-referenced probable-depression indicator for participant \(i\).

The primary estimand is the harmonized binary endpoint

\[ Y_i^{H} = \mathbb{1}\{\hat{\theta}_i \ge \tau^\ast\}, \]

where \(\hat{\theta}_i\) is the calibrated latent depression score from joint PHQ-SSQ modeling and \(\tau^\ast\) is the selected latent threshold linked to the PHQ-9 reference scale.

Secondary estimands include prevalence alignment and threshold transportability:

\[ \Delta_p = \Pr(Y_i^{H}=1) - \Pr(Y_i^{PHQ}=1), \]

where \(\Delta_p\) summarizes endpoint-prevalence drift under candidate thresholding strategies. Threshold comparison is not limited to a single rule. The anchored threshold linked to a PHQ-9 score of approximately 10 is compared with ROC-derived operating points and sensitivity- or specificity-targeted alternatives before a transport rule is selected.

A4 Study Design and Analytic Samples

The development sample comprises participants aged approximately 16-24 years with concurrent SSQ and PHQ observations available for joint calibration. The transport sample consists of SSQ-only records used to extend the harmonized scoring rule beyond the concurrent benchmark subset.

Eligibility is stage-specific:

  • structural-validity stages require complete item vectors for the candidate item set under evaluation;
  • IRT calibration requires valid item responses under the retained instrument configuration;
  • threshold comparison requires benchmark PHQ information;
  • endpoint assignment requires valid latent-score estimates from the final joint or fixed-parameter SSQ scoring workflow.

A5 Structural Modeling Strategy

Objective 2 evaluates three candidate SSQ configurations: SSQ-14, the theoretical SSQ-10 depressive subset, and the SSQ-8 comparison subset (Chibanda et al. 2019). Structural validity begins with Monte Carlo split-sample EFA using polychoric correlation structures and categorical-data retention criteria, followed by hold-out CFA using WLSMV estimation (Holgado–Tello et al. 2010; Wu and Estabrook 2016). Consensus item structures are identified across repeated split samples rather than from a single exploratory fit.

Let \(\mathbf{x}_i\) denote the ordinal or binary symptom vector and \(\theta_i\) the latent depression factor. The CFA measurement model is

\[ \mathbf{x}_i \sim f(\theta_i; \boldsymbol{\lambda}, \boldsymbol{\tau}), \]

where \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\) are factor-loading parameters and \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\) are item-threshold parameters. Fit diagnostics are interpreted jointly rather than through single-cutoff rules, consistent with the revised psychometric workflow (Hu and Bentler 1999; MacCallum et al. 2002). Measurement invariance is then assessed across sex and age strata so that downstream thresholding does not rest on item structures that vary materially across key subgroups (Putnick and Bornstein 2016; Vandenberg and Lance 2000).

A6 IRT Harmonization and Threshold Derivation

Harmonization uses graded response models implemented in mirt (Samejima 1969; Chalmers 2012). For item \(j\) and category threshold \(k\),

\[ \Pr(X_{ij} \ge k \mid \theta_i) = \operatorname{logit}^{-1}\left[a_j(\theta_i - b_{jk})\right]. \]

Separate instrument-level GRMs are used to confirm single-scale adequacy, after which a joint PHQ-SSQ calibration places both instruments on a common latent continuum. Latent trait scores are estimated using expected a posteriori scoring, and SSQ parameters from the joint model are exported as a fixed-parameter scoring engine so that SSQ-only records can be scored without refitting the full calibration model.

The primary applied threshold remains a clinically anchored \(\theta\) cutoff tied to the PHQ-9 test characteristic curve at approximately 10. However, the revised Objective 2 work compares this anchored rule with Youden-based ROC rules and constrained operating rules targeting high sensitivity or specificity before selecting the transportable endpoint definition for downstream Objectives 3-5.

A7 Performance Metrics and Uncertainty

Endpoint performance is summarized by:

  • sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, and accuracy;
  • AUC and Cohen’s \(\kappa\);
  • prevalence drift \(\Delta_p\);
  • subgroup consistency under alternate threshold strategies.

The workflow treats discrimination, calibration, and prevalence alignment as a joint decision problem rather than selecting thresholds on AUC or Youden performance alone. Uncertainty is summarized using resampling-based intervals and repeated-split diagnostics where appropriate (Efron and Tibshirani 1994).

A8 Planned Outputs and Reporting

Planned outputs are:

  1. item-retention and structural-validity diagnostics for candidate SSQ configurations;
  2. CFA, invariance, and DIF summaries for the retained SSQ-based depression structure;
  3. joint IRT calibration outputs and threshold-mapping comparisons;
  4. a fixed-parameter SSQ scoring engine for SSQ-only cohorts;
  5. the final harmonized depression endpoint carried forward into downstream Objectives 3-5.

Section B: Objective 3 - Social Determinant Trajectories SAP

B1 Background and Rationale

Objective 3 identifies developmental trajectories of childhood social determinants and evaluates how defensible those trajectories remain when longitudinal-information requirements are strengthened. This section therefore treats trajectory modeling as a scenario-comparison and reference-solution problem rather than as a search for a single intrinsically correct retained class solution (Muthén 2004; Nagin et al. 2016; Bauer and Curran 2003).

This framing follows the current harmonized trajectory workflow, which treats childhood social determinants as time-varying developmental processes and organizes them into early-childhood (0-5 years) and middle-late-childhood (6-12 years) windows (Hall and Posel 2019; Isabel Fortier et al. 2017; Simelane et al. 2025). The scientific aim is to preserve developmental timing while distinguishing robust trajectory structures from those that change materially when stronger repeated-measures requirements are imposed. Distal outcomes, including the harmonized depression endpoint and mediator variables, are used primarily for downstream class profiling after defensible reference solutions have been selected.

B3 Objectives and Estimands

The primary Objective 3 aim is to identify trajectory classes for key childhood social determinants across early childhood and middle-late childhood, and to compare retained class solutions across alternative longitudinal-information scenarios.

For model family \(a\), developmental window \(w\), and scenario \(s\), let \(C_{iaws}\) denote latent class membership with \(K_{aws}\) retained classes. The primary estimands include the retained class count \(K_{aws}\) and the class-proportion vector

\[ \boldsymbol{\pi}_{aws} = \left(\pi_{1aws}, \ldots, \pi_{K_{aws}aws}\right), \qquad \pi_{caws} = \Pr(C_{iaws}=c). \]

Secondary classification estimands include entropy and the mean top posterior probability

\[ \text{Entropy}_{aws}, \]

\[ \overline{p}^{\,top}_{aws} = \frac{1}{n_{aws}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{aws}} \max_c \hat{p}_{icaws}, \]

where \(\hat{p}_{icaws}\) is the posterior probability that participant \(i\) belongs to class \(c\) under model \((a,w,s)\).

Cross-scenario reallocation is summarized on the overlap sample

\[ \mathcal{O}_{awss'}=\left\{i: C_{iaws}\ \text{and}\ C_{iaws'}\ \text{are both observed}\right\}, \]

using the transition matrix

\[ T_{cc'}^{(awss')}= \Pr\left(C_{iaws'}=c' \mid C_{iaws}=c,\ i \in \mathcal{O}_{awss'}\right). \]

For distal profiling, the class-specific prevalence of binary distal variable \(D\) is

\[ \theta_{dcaws} = \Pr(D_i = 1 \mid C_{iaws}=c), \]

estimated descriptively from assigned or weighted class summaries, and from BCH-ready branches where available. These estimands are defined because the Objective 3 workflow compares unrestricted, restricted, and stricter restricted branches rather than relying on a single fitted branch as the sole evidence source.

B4 Study Design, Analysis Populations, and Exposure Definitions

The study uses a longitudinally linked childhood-exposure file nested within the AHRI Health and Demographic Surveillance System context, with linked adolescent follow-up information available for distal profiling. The childhood-exposure file contains 5398 unique children overall, with 4318 entering the early-childhood analytic cohort and 5386 entering the middle-late-childhood analytic cohort.

Exposure histories are organized into two pre-specified developmental windows: 0-5 years and 6-12 years. All trajectory models are built on harmonized repeated childhood measures, with emphasis on preserving developmental ordering and avoiding post hoc redefinition of windows or determinant families.

The active Objective 3 model family set is:

  • ULWOM: univariate maternal co-residence trajectories;
  • ULWOF: univariate paternal co-residence trajectories;
  • UHSES: univariate household socioeconomic status trajectories;
  • JPA: joint parental residence pattern trajectories;
  • JMAES: joint maternal absence plus household socioeconomic status trajectories.

The active indicator set therefore includes maternal absence, paternal absence, and ordinal household socioeconomic status. A candidate overcrowding indicator was evaluated during harmonization but was not retained in the reported analyses because the available coding could not support a defensible cutoff and household-member irregularities risked undercounting children in some households. Its exclusion is treated as a conservative measurement decision rather than a weakness in the retained trajectory analyses.

Analytic eligibility is scenario-specific. Let \(\mathcal{T}_w\) denote the age set in window \(w\), and let \(R_{itm}\) indicate whether determinant \(m\) is observed for child \(i\) at age \(t\).

For univariate models, the within-window observed-occasion count is

\[ O_{iwm}^{(uni)}=\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_w} R_{itm}, \]

and the three univariate scenarios are:

  1. Unrestricted: no additional minimum observed-occasion threshold beyond what contributes to the observed-data likelihood.
  2. Restricted (\(\ge 2\) occasions): retain children with \(O_{iwm}^{(uni)} \ge 2\).
  3. Restricted (\(\ge 3\) occasions): retain children with \(O_{iwm}^{(uni)} \ge 3\).

For joint models, a counted occasion requires all indicators entering the joint state to be observed at that age. If \(\mathcal{M}_j\) denotes the indicator set in joint model \(j\), the within-window complete-occasion count is

\[ O_{iwj}^{(joint)}=\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_w}\prod_{m \in \mathcal{M}_j} R_{itm}, \]

and the three joint scenarios are:

  1. Unrestricted (\(\ge 1\) complete occasion): retain children with \(O_{iwj}^{(joint)} \ge 1\).
  2. Restricted (\(\ge 2\) complete occasions): retain children with \(O_{iwj}^{(joint)} \ge 2\).
  3. Stricter restricted (\(\ge 3\) complete occasions): retain children with \(O_{iwj}^{(joint)} \ge 3\).

B5 LCGA and Joint-Trajectory Strategy

Within each developmental window, the repeated ages are recoded to a local time score

\[ \tau_t = t - \min(\mathcal{T}_w). \]

For each process \(m\) in class \(c\), the class-specific latent growth mean is

\[ \eta_{cm}(\tau_t)=I_{cm}+S_{cm}\tau_t+Q_{cm}\tau_t^2, \]

where \(I_{cm}\), \(S_{cm}\), and \(Q_{cm}\) are class-specific intercept, linear slope, and quadratic slope means. The baseline LCGA specification fixes all within-class growth-factor variances and covariances to zero, including cross-process growth-factor covariances in joint models.

Maternal absence and paternal absence are modeled as categorical repeated indicators under a probit-threshold parameterization:

\[ \Pr(Y_{itm}=1 \mid C_i=c) = \Phi\left(\eta_{cm}(\tau_t)-\kappa_{tm}\right). \]

Household socioeconomic status is modeled as an ordinal repeated indicator with levels low, middle, and high:

\[ \Pr(Y_{itm}^{SES} \le k \mid C_i=c) = \Phi\left(\kappa_{tmk}-\eta_{cm}(\tau_t)\right), \qquad k=0,1. \]

In joint models, a common latent class variable is shared across processes, but each process retains its own class-specific growth function. The JPA model combines maternal and paternal absence, whereas the JMAES model combines maternal absence and ordinal household socioeconomic status. Models are estimated in Mplus using TYPE = MIXTURE, ESTIMATOR = MLR, ALGORITHM = INTEGRATION, and INTEGRATION = MONTECARLO (Muthén 2004; Nagin et al. 2016).

B6 Class Enumeration and Model Selection

Class enumeration is conducted sequentially from \(K=1\) upward, with the current implementation permitting enumeration up to \(K=15\) unless the minimum class threshold is breached earlier. Class-enumeration decisions combine statistical and scientific criteria:

  • information criteria and neighboring class-count stability;
  • minimum useful class size and posterior separation;
  • substantive interpretability of class shapes;
  • consistency of retained solutions across analytic restrictions;
  • documentation against GRoLTS and SMART-LCA-aligned reporting standards (Nylund et al. 2007; Masyn 2013; Lissa et al. 2024).

The default minimum class threshold is \(5\%\) of the analytic sample for that model-window-scenario. When possible, the decision set includes the selected \(K\) and adjacent \(K+1\) solution for review, with lower adjacent solutions retained when \(K+1\) is unavailable, collapses, or fails admissibility criteria.

Because mixture solutions are sensitive to specification, local maxima, and sample restriction, retained solutions are also evaluated under structural sensitivity checks (Bauer and Curran 2003; Bauer 2007; Hipp and Bauer 2006; Watson et al. 2022). The current structural-sensitivity profiles are:

  1. strict LCGA: all within-class growth-factor variances and covariances fixed to zero;
  2. free intercept and slope variances within class;
  3. free intercept and slope variances plus intercept-slope covariance within class.

B7 Cross-Scenario Comparison, Distal Profiling, and Missing Data

Cross-scenario comparison is treated as a core part of the Objective 3 inference rather than as a post hoc sensitivity appendix. For each model family and developmental window, the SAP compares:

  • analytic sample size;
  • retained class count;
  • entropy;
  • mean top posterior probability;
  • posterior range across classes;
  • minimum class proportion.

Class reallocation is summarized using overlap-sample transition matrices, same-label retention proportions, and alluvial diagrams built on the overlap sample. Because overlap-based alluvia quantify reallocation only among participants represented in all compared scenarios, total scenario-specific attrition is reported separately in summary tables and text rather than inferred from the alluvia alone.

Each model family and developmental window will have one defensible reference solution selected for downstream profiling. Under the current design, the default candidate reference branches are:

  1. univariate: restricted (\(\ge 3\) occasions);
  2. joint: stricter restricted (\(\ge 3\) complete occasions).

The distal-outcome layer is descriptive by default. Planned class profiles include probable depression, mediator variables, and contextual descriptive variables where relevant. Where model outputs support BCH weighting, BCH-based distal summaries may be reported as secondary analyses for branch-ready reference solutions.

Within each eligible scenario, repeated childhood outcomes are handled under the observed-data likelihood of the fitted mixture model. The restricted scenarios therefore change analytic eligibility rather than changing the missing-data model itself (Schafer and Graham 2002; Graham 2009; Parsons et al. 2024).

B8 Planned Outputs and Reporting

Planned outputs include:

  1. full class-enumeration summaries across all active model families, windows, and scenarios;
  2. retained-solution metric tables comparing sample size, class count, entropy, mean top posterior probability, and posterior range;
  3. trajectory-profile figures, class-transition tables, and scenario-reallocation figures;
  4. structural-sensitivity and reporting-checklist artifacts aligned with GRoLTS and SMART-LCA;
  5. descriptive distal-prevalence summaries and BCH-based secondary distal outputs where available.

The main manuscript emphasizes analytic framework, scenario comparison, retained-solution defensibility, and key class-reallocation figures. The Supplementary Material carries the fuller class-enumeration ladders, expanded transition tables, extended trajectory panels, distal-prevalence tables, and audit artifacts. The results hub and trajectory comparison notebooks function as the full staging surface from which main-text and supplementary outputs are extracted.

B10 Consistency Stamp (Objective 3)

Consistency stamp date: 2026-05-25 Scope: Objective 3 (Social Determinant Trajectories) only.

This consistency stamp records the intended SAP scope for the current Objective 3 specification:

  1. the active Objective 3 family set is constrained to ULWOM, ULWOF, UHSES, JPA, and JMAES;
  2. univariate scenario comparisons are defined as Unrestricted, Restricted (\(\ge 2\) occasions), and Restricted (\(\ge 3\) occasions);
  3. joint scenario comparisons are defined as Unrestricted (\(\ge 1\) complete occasion), Restricted (\(\ge 2\) complete occasions), and Stricter restricted (\(\ge 3\) complete occasions);

  5. defensible reference solutions are selected before distal profiling, with BCH-based distal analyses treated as secondary where branch-ready outputs are available.

Section C: Objective 4 - Behavioral Mediators SAP

C1 Background and Rationale

Objective 4 identifies and prioritizes adolescent behavioral and proximal social pathway variables linking childhood social determinants to adolescent depressive symptoms. This section defines Objective 4 as a pathway-screening and mediator-prioritization analysis. It is designed to decide which pathway domains should be carried forward into later mediation-focused work, while keeping the current inferential target distinct from formal direct-effect or indirect-effect decomposition. This distinction matters in a harmonized multi-cohort platform where pathway variables, endpoint timing, and contextual exposure history all vary across source studies (Jörns-Presentati et al. 2021; Jakobsson et al. 2024; Rossouw et al. 2024).

Objective 4 treats the adolescent pathway block as a constrained but policy-relevant subset of a broader locally grounded pathway ecology. Candidate domains include material deprivation, violence, school-related adversity, social connectedness, and sexual-risk behavior, all positioned after childhood exposure windows and before the harmonized depressive-symptom endpoint (Mkhize et al. 2024; Partap et al. 2023; Zimmerman et al. 2021).

C3 Objectives and Estimands

The Objective 4 study objective is to identify and prioritize adolescent behavioral and proximal social pathway variables linking childhood social determinants to adolescent depressive symptoms. The primary estimands are pathway-screening estimands derived from the joint pattern of upstream A-path support and downstream B-path evidence.

For pathway variable \(M_j\), the A-path model is

\[ \operatorname{logit}\left\{P(M_j = 1 \mid S_{0-5}, S_{6-12}, C)\right\} = \alpha_{0j} + \alpha_{1j}^{\top}S_{0-5} + \alpha_{2j}^{\top}S_{6-12} + \alpha_{3j}^{\top} C. \]

For the depressive-symptom endpoint, the B-path model is

\[ \operatorname{logit}\left\{P(Y_i^H=1 \mid S_{0-5}, S_{6-12}, M_1,\ldots,M_7, C)\right\} = \beta_0 + \beta_1^{\top}S_{0-5} + \beta_2^{\top}S_{6-12} + \sum_{j=1}^{7}\theta_j M_j + \beta_3^{\top} C, \]

where \(S_{0-5}\) and \(S_{6-12}\) denote childhood class-history representations, \(M_j\) denotes a candidate adolescent pathway domain, and \(C\) denotes the fixed covariate set. The primary B-path parameter for each pathway domain is \(\theta_j\), interpreted as the pathway-specific association with the harmonized depression endpoint after conditioning on class history and fixed covariates.

The principal study output is a prioritization judgement for each pathway domain based on upstream A-path support, downstream B-path support, robustness to fixed contextual adjustment, and coherence with the contextual pathway inventory.

C4 Mediator Definitions and Coding

The empirically testable adolescent pathway block comprises seven harmonized binary domains:

  • alcohol use;
  • ever having had sexual intercourse;
  • food insecurity;
  • lack of government grant receipt;
  • violence exposure;
  • low social support;
  • school disengagement.

These variables are treated as candidate pathway indicators rather than as already-identified formal mediators. They are harmonized from adolescent data collected after the childhood exposure windows and before the harmonized depression endpoint. Fixed covariate adjustment includes sex, adolescent age, rural residence, and calendar year of outcome measurement (Marlow et al. 2023; I. Fortier et al. 2017; Chilver et al. 2023; Mthiyane et al. 2025).

C5 Modeling Strategy

Objective 4 carries forward paired two-window class histories rather than using isolated class snapshots. The retained model families are:

  • ULWOM: univariate lived without mother;
  • UHSES: univariate household socioeconomic status;
  • JMAES: joint maternal absence and household socioeconomic status.

The strict restricted JMAES branch uses five early-childhood classes and seven middle-late-childhood classes. These families are interpreted as distinct but related representations of childhood social-determinant history. They are not pooled into a single exposure model; consistency across families contributes to pathway prioritization.

The class-history backbone is written as

\[ P(S_{0-5}, S_{6-12}, U^{(0-5)}, U^{(6-12)}) = P(S_{0-5}) \times P(S_{6-12}) \times \prod_t P(U_t^{(0-5)} \mid S_{0-5}) \times \prod_t P(U_t^{(6-12)} \mid S_{6-12}), \]

with posterior class uncertainty carried into downstream screening rather than resolved through deterministic single-class assignment (Vermunt 2010; Asparouhov and Muthén 2014).

Within that class-history framework, three analytic layers are prespecified:

  1. A-path screening of pathway-variable probabilities as functions of childhood class history and fixed covariates;
  2. B-path models relating pathway domains to the harmonized depression endpoint after adjustment for childhood class history and fixed covariates;
  3. jointly adjusted pathway models used to rank candidate pathway domains rather than to claim identified indirect effects.

All downstream regression stages use posterior-probability-weighted logistic models with participant-level cluster-robust standard errors.

C6 Mediator Prioritization and Phenotype Planning

Pathway prioritization is based on the joint pattern of:

  • A-path support across pre-specified primary and secondary hypothesis tiers;
  • B-path support across the three retained model families;
  • attenuation under nested fixed-covariate adjustment;
  • substantive plausibility in relation to the broader co-created pathway ecology.

Within that framework, food insecurity is the clearest candidate pathway, with violence exposure also prioritized and ever having had sexual intercourse retained as a supporting pathway domain. Alcohol use, lack of government grant receipt, low social support, and school disengagement remain part of the full evaluated pathway block and are reported transparently as lower-priority or weakly supported domains rather than removed from the screening record (Duby et al. 2022; Mkhize et al. 2022).

C7 Sensitivity and Robustness

Objective 4 places particular emphasis on nested fixed-covariate adjustment. Sensitivity analyses will assess whether attenuation of pathway-depression associations is driven primarily by age, calendar year, rural residence, or combinations of these factors. This reflects the design assumption that developmental timing and secular period are part of the data-generating structure rather than optional nuisance adjustments.

Additional robustness layers include:

  • pathway ranking by pre-specified primary and secondary A-path hypothesis tiers rather than nominal significance alone (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995);
  • complete-case main analyses with a separate 20-imputation maternal-baseline diagnostic branch retained as supporting specification work (White et al. 2011; Sterne et al. 2009);
  • interaction reviews for selected pathway combinations, especially violence exposure with school disengagement;
  • a contextual pathway inventory used to assess local relevance, partial measurement, and measurement gaps;
  • a working mediation-specification DAG used to summarize how the screening outputs inform later mediation-focused modelling;
  • reporting aligned to STROBE and AGReMA guidance (Elm et al. 2007; Moreno-Betancur et al. 2021).

The maternal-baseline imputation branch and working DAG do not replace the main screening estimands. They provide supporting evidence for later mediation specification by distinguishing stronger imputed maternal base candidates from auxiliary imputation variables and by separating baseline covariates, fixed contextual covariates, screened pathway domains, and broader conceptual downstream structural nodes.

Section D: Objective 5 - Causal Mediation SAP

D1 Background and Rationale

Objective 5 carries forward the retained childhood exposure histories, the harmonized depressive-symptom endpoint, and the mediator shortlist from Objectives 2-4 into formal causal mediation analysis. The purpose is no longer pathway screening alone, but estimation of how much depressive-symptom burden is associated with selected childhood exposure histories overall, how much remains outside the selected adolescent pathways, and how much is carried through the retained mediator set (Pearl 2001; Imai et al. 2010; Valeri and VanderWeele 2013; VanderWeele et al. 2016).

The finalized Objective 5 implementation is anchored on the strict joint-history branch requiring at least three observed childhood occasions per developmental window. The primary exposure is the joint maternal-absence plus household socioeconomic history because it preserves developmental ordering, represents co-occurring relational and material adversity, and yielded the richest defensible joint trajectory structure. Supportive univariate maternal-absence and household socioeconomic histories are analyzed within the same joint-history analytical sample.

D2 Objectives and Estimands

The primary Objective 5 objective is to estimate total effects, direct effects, mediator-specific indirect effects, and combined-set indirect effects linking childhood exposure histories to adolescent depressive symptoms.

Let \(H_i\) denote the childhood exposure history for participant \(i\), with \(h_0\) the reference history and \(h_1\) the comparator history; let \(Y_i\) denote the binary harmonized depressive-symptom endpoint; let \(M_{ij}\) denote mediator \(j\); and let \(\mathbf{M}_i=(FDSC_i, VLNC_i, ESXC_i)\) denote the joint three-mediator block.

The primary estimands are:

\[ \text{TE} = E\{Y_i(h_1, M(h_1)) - Y_i(h_0, M(h_0))\}, \]

\[ \text{NIE}_j = E\{Y_i(h_1, M_j(h_1)) - Y_i(h_1, M_j(h_0))\}, \]

\[ \text{Block NDE} = E\{Y_i(h_1, \mathbf{M}(h_0)) - Y_i(h_0, \mathbf{M}(h_0))\}, \]

and

\[ \text{Block NIE} = E\{Y_i(h_1, \mathbf{M}(h_1)) - Y_i(h_1, \mathbf{M}(h_0))\}. \]

All reported effects are summarized on the risk-difference scale. The direct effect reported in the main manuscript corresponds to the combined-set block direct effect, because single-mediator direct effects vary by mediator model and are not directly comparable across separate single-mediator decompositions.

D3 Exposure, Outcome, Mediators, and Covariates

The retained Objective 5 exposure structures are:

  • primary joint maternal absence + household socioeconomic history: 5 early-childhood classes and 8 middle-late-childhood classes under the strict >=3 branch;
  • secondary univariate maternal-absence history: 4 early-childhood classes and 5 middle-late-childhood classes;
  • secondary univariate household socioeconomic history: 4 early-childhood classes and 7 middle-late-childhood classes.

The outcome is the harmonized binary depressive-symptom endpoint from Objective 2. The core mediator set carried into the main decomposition comprises:

  • food insecurity;
  • violence exposure; and
  • ever having had sexual intercourse.

The extension pathway record remains available for:

  • alcohol use;
  • lack of government grant receipt;
  • low social support; and
  • school disengagement.

Two adjustment sets are prespecified:

  • Base causal mediation estimand: sex, maternal age, and maternal education;
  • Context-adjusted mediation estimand: sex, maternal age, maternal education, adolescent age, rural residence, and calendar year.

D4 Analysis Population and Missing Data

The primary joint-history analytical sample consists of participants with:

  • an observed harmonized depressive-symptom endpoint;
  • contribution to the selected strict joint transition-history bundle; and
  • valid baseline information for the imputation and inferential stages.

The finalized Objective 5 implementation used an overlap sample of 1381 participants. Maternal baseline covariates were multiply imputed using 20 imputations with a maximum of 20 iterations and seed 20260414. Posterior-probability-weighted pseudo-records were then carried forward into the inferential models so that class uncertainty was retained rather than collapsed into deterministic class assignment.

D5 Estimation Strategy

Objective 5 uses transition-history g-computation on the probability scale. Total effects are estimated first for prespecified contrasts between selected childhood exposure histories. Mediator-specific decompositions then fit:

\[ \Pr(M_{ij}=1 \mid H_i, \mathbf{C}_i) \]

through a logistic mediator model and

\[ \Pr(Y_i=1 \mid H_i, M_{ij}, \mathbf{C}_i) \]

through a corresponding logistic outcome model. Standardized counterfactual means are combined to obtain total effects, direct effects, and mediator-specific indirect effects on the risk-difference scale.

For the combined three-mediator set, the joint mediator distribution is represented through sequential factorization rather than by summing separate indirect effects. This is necessary because mediator-specific indirect effects are not additive when mediators are correlated or represent overlapping components of the same adolescent pathway ecology (VanderWeele 2016).

The finalized pipeline used:

  • 300 coefficient simulation draws for total-effect uncertainty propagation;
  • 120 joint-block simulation draws for the combined three-mediator decomposition;
  • 0 expanded-block simulation draws in the final retained pipeline.

D6 Prespecified Contrast Families

The primary total-effect contrasts compare adverse joint histories against lower-risk reference histories:

  • early childhood: Low Maternal Absence, Improving SES as the reference history;
  • middle-late childhood: Low Maternal Absence, Higher SES as the reference history.

The strongest prespecified contrast is the middle-late childhood comparison of High Maternal Absence, Low SES versus Low Maternal Absence, Higher SES. This is the focal contrast for main decomposition reporting because it showed the clearest adverse total-effect signal in the completed analyses.

Supportive univariate contrasts are prespecified around lower-risk and chronic-adversity anchors within the strict maternal-absence and household socioeconomic histories, and are interpreted as one-domain simplifications of the main childhood exposure story rather than replacements for the joint-history estimand.

D7 Robustness and Interpretation Rules

Objective 5 interpretation is intentionally conservative. Causal interpretation requires no unmeasured confounding of the exposure-outcome, exposure-mediator, and mediator-outcome relations after conditioning on the measured covariates (Imai et al. 2010; VanderWeele 2016). Because these assumptions are demanding in observational data, the SAP applies the following rules:

  • positive total effects indicate higher depressive-symptom risk in the comparator history than in the reference history;
  • indirect effects are treated as pathway-consistent evidence when directionally coherent with the hypothesized mediator pathway;
  • indirect effects whose confidence intervals include 0 are interpreted as suggestive rather than conclusive;
  • context-adjusted estimates are stability checks on the base estimand rather than replacements for it;
  • secondary univariate analyses are supportive and do not displace the primary joint-history interpretation.

The finalized diagnostics showed stable model completion across imputations and adjustment sets, with total-effect and most mediator models using 1381 participants and 9347 pseudo-rows, dropping to 1365 participants and 9238 pseudo-rows when ever having had sexual intercourse entered the base joint mediator block.

D8 Planned Outputs and Reporting

Planned Objective 5 outputs include:

  1. total-effect contrasts for the primary joint-history exposure and the supportive univariate exposure histories;
  2. mediator-specific indirect-effect decompositions for food insecurity, violence exposure, and ever having had sexual intercourse;
  3. combined-set direct and total indirect effects for the joint three-mediator block;
  4. context-adjusted counterparts for the main total-effect and decomposition analyses;
  5. model diagnostics and implementation manifests documenting imputation, simulation, and convergence.
