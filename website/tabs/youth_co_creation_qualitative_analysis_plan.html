Youth Co-Creation Qualitative Analysis Plan

Qualitative analysis plan for the Photovoice-based youth co-creation component of CO-LUMINATE.

Overview

This document describes the qualitative analysis plan for the CO-LUMINATE youth co-creation component. It sits alongside the Statistical Analysis Plan, which covers the quantitative analyses for Objectives 2-5. The qualitative plan specifies how Photovoice materials, workshop narratives, member-checking outputs, and youth-informed pathway maps are generated, analysed, validated, and translated into interpretive inputs for the wider project.

The current release covers:

  • Section A: Purpose, rationale, and study design
  • Section B: Data sources, timeline, and analytic objectives
  • Section C: Thematic analysis and pathway mapping
  • Section D: Participatory validation, ethics, and trustworthiness
  • Section E: Outputs, constraints, and reporting decisions

Section A: Purpose, Rationale, and Study Design

A1 Purpose

The purpose of this plan is to document the analytic approach for the Photovoice-based youth co-creation component of CO-LUMINATE. The work has two connected roles:

  1. To produce a youth-informed conceptual and pathway framework for understanding how social determinants shape young people’s mental health in the AHRI Health and Demographic Surveillance System setting.
  2. To provide contextual grounding for later quantitative analyses, especially pathway screening, causal diagram development, results interpretation, and stakeholder-facing dissemination.

The plan is not designed to create a separate statistical dataset or to estimate population prevalence. Its central product is an interpretive pathway inventory and causal meaning-making framework grounded in the photographs, narratives, and workshop interpretations of youth co-creators.

A2 Background and Rationale

CO-LUMINATE examines how social determinants shape adolescent and youth mental health in rural KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. The quantitative component links harmonized mental health measures to longitudinal HDSS data. Secondary data alone, however, cannot fully determine which social determinants matter locally, how they are experienced, or which mechanisms young people see as linking social conditions to mental health.

Objective 1 addresses this gap through youth co-creation. Youth co-creators use Photovoice, group narrative-building, and participatory pathway mapping to identify locally meaningful determinants and causal mechanisms. This approach reduces the distance between technical analytic decisions and the lived realities of the young people whose communities and life-course contexts are represented in the CO-LUMINATE data.

The analysis is grounded in two complementary orientations. First, it uses a critical realist orientation, treating social determinants as operating through context-specific generative mechanisms. Second, it uses a social constructionist orientation, recognizing that accounts of community, risk, support, and mental health are produced through language, relationships, and power. Together, these orientations support an analysis that is pathway-oriented, participatory, and attentive to local meaning.

A3 Study Design

The qualitative work is a participatory youth co-creation study using Photovoice. It is best described as deep participatory co-creation within a larger mixed-methods program. The broader CO-LUMINATE question and quantitative design were developed before youth co-creators joined the study. Youth co-creators therefore did not originate the full study design, but they hold substantive interpretive authority over their photographs, narratives, themes, and causal pathway representations.

The work has two analytic phases:

  • Pre-secondary analysis phase: develop a youth-informed inventory of social determinants, mechanisms, and causal pathways before formal interpretation of quantitative results.
  • Post-secondary analysis phase: use youth interpretation workshops after quantitative outputs are available to contextualize findings, identify what measured variables miss, and support accessible dissemination.

A4 Participants and Setting

The study was conducted in the AHRI HDSS in the Mtubatuba Local Municipality within the Hlabisa Health Sub-District, uMkhanyakude, KwaZulu-Natal. Activities were hosted at AHRI’s Somkhele campus.

The implemented co-creation group included 11 youth co-creators aged 18-24 years living in the AHRI HDSS area. Recruitment took place from 5 September 2025 to 12 September 2025 using word of mouth, snowball sampling, WhatsApp adverts, and recruitment across diverse geographic clusters within the HDSS. The group included six males and five females; ten participants had finished school, three were parents, and none were employed at the time the research took place.

The original proposal anticipated approximately ten youth advisory group members aged 13-24. The implemented Photovoice component therefore focused on young adults aged 18-24, and this distinction should be stated clearly in reporting.

Section B: Data Sources, Timeline, and Analytic Objectives

B1 Data Sources

The qualitative corpus comprises:

  • photographs generated by youth co-creators during the Photovoice walkabout;
  • selected and titled photographs presented by youth co-creators during narrative-building workshops;
  • audio recordings and live notes from narrative-building discussions;
  • facilitator notes and analytic memos;
  • handwritten member-checking notes in isiZulu or English;
  • the thematic codebook and coded data managed in qualitative analysis software;
  • the pathway mapping spreadsheet used to document causal chains;
  • the final overview causal diagram and supporting pathway list;
  • later interpretation-workshop outputs, once quantitative results are available.

The data are qualitative and participatory. Photographs, captions, workshop narratives, and pathway notes are analysed together rather than as independent units.

B2 Activity Timeline

Activity Date(s) Analytic role
Recruitment and onboarding 5-12 September 2025 Establish youth co-creator group from the HDSS communities
Inception workshop 16 September 2025 Build shared understanding of adolescent mental health, social determinants, roles, risks, benefits, and Photovoice ethics
Community walkabout 22 September 2025 Generate photographs of physical, social, and symbolic determinants of youth mental health
Narrative-building workshops 23 September, 14 October, and 17 October 2025 Elicit photo titles, meanings, local explanations, and group reflections
Member-checking and interpretation workshops 28 November and 4 December 2025 Refine themes, check meanings, validate pathways, and develop causal representations
Post-secondary interpretation workshops After quantitative outputs are available Interpret quantitative findings, identify alignments and gaps, and support dissemination

B3 Analytic Objectives

The qualitative analysis has five objectives:

  1. Identify social determinants of mental health that youth co-creators see as locally salient.
  2. Characterize how these determinants operate through risk and protective pathways.
  3. Distinguish root causes, intervening mechanisms, and mental health outcomes in youth-defined causal accounts.
  4. Translate qualitative pathway accounts into a structured pathway inventory that can inform quantitative variable selection, DAG development, and interpretation.
  5. Document the co-creation process as a methodological contribution for future youth-centred mixed-methods research in HDSS settings.

B4 Research Questions

The analysis will answer the following questions:

  1. Which social determinants do youth co-creators identify as shaping young people’s mental health in the AHRI HDSS setting?
  2. What negative and protective mechanisms do youth co-creators describe between these determinants and mental health outcomes?
  3. Which determinants and mechanisms are captured, partially captured, or absent in the current CO-LUMINATE secondary data platform?
  4. How should youth co-creator interpretations inform causal diagrams, pathway-screening decisions, and interpretation of quantitative results?
  5. What good practices can be documented for co-creating an analytic framework with young people in an under-resourced rural setting?

Section C: Thematic Analysis and Pathway Mapping

C1 Analytic Orientation

The analysis uses a hybrid inductive-deductive thematic approach. Deductive coding is guided by established social determinants of mental health frameworks, including domains such as socioeconomic position, poverty, education, employment, housing, early childhood development, social support, exclusion, physical environment, basic services, violence, safety, and governance. Inductive coding remains open to locally salient domains not well represented in standard frameworks.

Themes are treated as interpretive constructs that capture shared patterns of meaning, not as simple frequency counts. Frequency of discussion may help describe salience, but it will not be used as the sole basis for importance. A determinant may be analytically important because it clarifies a mechanism, reveals a local meaning, or exposes a gap in the secondary data even if it appears less often.

C2 Coding Framework

The initial deductive framework includes the following domains:

Deductive domain Use in analysis
Socioeconomic position, poverty, and economic inequality Capture material deprivation, income instability, food stress, grant reliance, livelihood loss, and inequality
Education, employment, and working conditions Capture schooling conditions, youth development, employment access, unemployment, and work-related opportunity structures
Housing and living conditions Capture housing quality, sanitation, household safety, water access, and household-level discomfort or shame
Early childhood development Capture creche access, early learning, safety, food provision, and development foundations
Social support, social capital, exclusion, and discrimination Capture family support, community cohesion, stigma, social exclusion, youth voice, and access to shared resources
Physical environment Capture pollution, mining effects, climate stress, land damage, dumping, agriculture, and environmental hazards
Access to basic services Capture water, electricity, healthcare, policing, infrastructure, transport, and service dignity
Violence, crime, and safety Capture interpersonal violence, domestic violence, unsafe spaces, taverns, substance-related insecurity, and environmental hazards
Governance and policy Retain as a sensitizing concept, but do not force-code where youth narratives do not directly support it
Culture, religion, and spirituality Capture church support, pastoral counselling, rituals, ancestral connection, spiritual conflict, and stigma

Inductive codes may be added when youth co-creators describe a pattern that is not adequately captured by the deductive framework. Examples include institutional neglect, survival orientation, symbolic meanings of service failure, and tensions between religious and traditional spiritual systems.

C3 Thematic Analysis Procedure

The thematic analysis will follow six phases adapted from reflexive thematic analysis:

  1. Familiarisation: review photographs, titles, transcripts, notes, and workshop discussions in their full context.
  2. Initial coding: code photo and narrative content using both the deductive social determinants framework and inductively generated codes.
  3. Theme development: group related codes into candidate themes that represent shared meaning across images and narratives.
  4. Theme review: compare candidate themes against the full corpus, checking whether each theme is coherent, distinct, and supported.
  5. Theme definition and naming: refine theme names, analytic scope, and boundaries, prioritizing language that preserves youth co-creators’ intended meanings.
  6. Analytic write-up: produce a thematic narrative with exemplar photographs, captions, and selected short excerpts where appropriate and ethically permitted.

The current analysis retains nine substantive thematic domains:

  • access to basic services;
  • violence, crime, and safety;
  • socioeconomic position, poverty, and economic inequality;
  • social support, social capital, exclusion, and discrimination;
  • physical environment;
  • culture, religion, and spirituality;
  • education, employment, and working conditions;
  • housing and living conditions;
  • early childhood development.

These domains should remain the primary thematic structure unless later member checking or post-secondary interpretation work requires revision.

C4 Pathway Mapping Procedure

After thematic coding, a second analytic pass identifies causal chains linking determinants to mental health outcomes. Each pathway is entered into a structured pathway matrix with the following fields:

Field Description
Pathway ID Stable identifier for each youth-defined pathway
Thematic domain Primary determinant domain
Root cause or exposure Social, structural, environmental, or relational condition described by youth co-creators
Intervening mechanism(s) Psychological, social, behavioural, service, or material process linking exposure to mental health
Direction Risk, protective, or mixed
Outcome Poor mental health, positive mental health, or both
Evidence source Photograph, caption, narrative workshop, member-checking note, or supplementary pathway list
Secondary-data capture Directly captured, partially captured, not directly captured, or interpretive context only
Quantitative implication Relevance for variable selection, DAG development, interpretation, or dissemination

The supplementary pathway list currently includes 45 youth-defined causal pathways. These pathways will not be treated as ranked or weighted unless a later participatory exercise explicitly asks youth co-creators to assign importance, strength, or priority. Until then, the pathways should be reported as a structured qualitative inventory rather than as a quantitative hierarchy.

The overview causal diagram should classify concepts as:

  • root causes or upstream determinants;
  • intervening causes or mechanisms;
  • risk pathways;
  • protective pathways;
  • poor mental health outcomes;
  • positive mental health outcomes.

This diagram will be used as a bridge into formal DAG development for the quantitative component. The diagram should not be treated as a final statistical DAG without additional causal-structure review by the quantitative team.

C5 Link to Quantitative Analysis

The qualitative analysis contributes to CO-LUMINATE through a pathway inventory rather than a participant-level analytic dataset. Its main function is to make explicit which youth-defined mechanisms are measured in the current secondary data, which are only partially measured, and which remain outside the available data.

Youth-informed pathway domain Current secondary-data relationship Main analytic use
Food insecurity and material deprivation Directly captured through food insecurity and related material-deprivation measures Supports pathway-screening and interpretation of material stress
Violence exposure and unsafe environments Directly captured through violence exposure measures where available Supports modelling and interpretation of insecurity-related distress
Alcohol use and substance-related risk Directly captured through alcohol use measures where available Supports behavioural mediator screening
Sexual debut and broader adolescent risk ecology Partially captured through sexual debut or sexual behaviour indicators Interpreted as one marker within a wider risk ecology
Social protection and grant conditions Partially captured through government grant receipt or lack of receipt Interpreted cautiously because grants may signal protection, strain, or deprivation
School climate and educational pathway conditions Not directly harmonized in the current pathway block Retained as contextual explanation and future measurement priority
Family separation and migration-related stress Not directly harmonized in the current pathway block Retained as contextual explanation and potential future pathway domain
Housing quality and water insecurity Not directly harmonized in the current pathway block Retained as a key unmeasured mechanism
Social connectedness and community inclusion or exclusion Not directly harmonized in the current pathway block Retained as interpretation context for social capital and help-seeking
Service access, care barriers, and stigma Not directly harmonized in the current pathway block Retained as context for healthcare access and distress interpretation
Religion, spirituality, and cultural support or strain Not directly harmonized in the current pathway block Retained as a locally salient protective and risk domain

This mapping should be updated whenever the harmonized quantitative data platform changes.

Section D: Participatory Validation, Ethics, and Trustworthiness

D1 Member Checking and Participatory Validation

Member checking is a core analytic step rather than an optional dissemination activity. Youth co-creators review thematic summaries, pathway interpretations, and causal diagrams to confirm that the analysis preserves intended meanings and does not impose external interpretations that contradict their accounts.

Member-checking workshops should document:

  • areas of agreement with the draft themes and pathways;
  • changes requested by youth co-creators;
  • disagreements or divergent interpretations;
  • themes or pathways that youth co-creators consider missing;
  • wording changes needed to preserve local meaning;
  • decisions about which photographs or excerpts may be used publicly;
  • youth co-creators’ interpretation of the final overview diagram.

Where youth co-creators disagree with the research team’s interpretation, the youth interpretation should be prioritized for photographs and narratives generated by them. Researcher analytic memos may document alternative interpretations, but should not override youth co-creators’ stated meanings.

D2 Post-Secondary Interpretation Phase

After quantitative analyses are available, youth co-creators should be invited to structured interpretation workshops. These workshops will focus on aggregate findings only and will not expose individual-level participant data.

The interpretation workshops should ask:

  1. Which quantitative findings feel consistent with youth co-creators’ lived understanding of the community?
  2. Which findings feel surprising, incomplete, or potentially misleading without context?
  3. Which unmeasured pathways from the qualitative inventory help explain the quantitative results?
  4. Which messages should be prioritized for young people, families, community stakeholders, services, and policy actors?
  5. Which photographs, diagrams, or plain-language materials best support dissemination?

The output of this phase should be a structured interpretation memo linking quantitative findings to youth co-creator reflections. This memo can support the website, manuscripts, presentations, infographics, and the Photovoice exhibition or townhall dissemination event.

D3 Ethics, Safeguarding, and Data Protection

The co-creation component received ethical approval from the University of KwaZulu-Natal Biomedical Research Ethics Committee, reference 00009064/2025. Youth co-creators provided written informed consent.

The Photovoice process includes specific ethical protections:

  • avoid identifiable faces in photographs unless appropriate consent is in place;
  • obtain consent before photographing other people;
  • avoid public disclosure of sensitive or identifying information;
  • discuss benefits, risks, roles, and safeguards during onboarding;
  • conduct activities in ways that respond to youth safeguarding needs;
  • use group walkabouts and transport arrangements to reduce physical safety risks;
  • conduct interpretation and translation checks across isiZulu and English to preserve meaning.

Any public-facing use of photographs, captions, or excerpts should be reviewed against consent, identifiability, sensitivity, and youth co-creator preferences before release.

D4 Reflexivity and Power

The analysis recognizes power differences between academic researchers and youth co-creators. Academic researchers contribute methodological, analytic, and institutional expertise. Youth co-creators contribute proximal knowledge of community life, social conditions, and the meanings attached to photographs and narratives.

To reduce extractive interpretation, the analysis will use:

  • explicit reflexive memos about facilitator assumptions and framing effects;
  • transparent documentation of deductive frameworks used by the research team;
  • independent coding followed by cross-checking and discussion;
  • member checking with youth co-creators;
  • co-authorship and acknowledgement practices where appropriate;
  • youth approval of final themes, pathways, and selected public-facing materials.

D5 Trustworthiness

Trustworthiness will be supported through:

  • Credibility: member checking, interpretation workshops, use of photographs and narratives together, and attention to disconfirming or divergent accounts.
  • Dependability: documentation of coding decisions, pathway-mapping rules, workshop dates, and changes made after member checking.
  • Confirmability: reflexive memos, preservation of an audit trail, and transparent separation between youth interpretation and researcher synthesis.
  • Transferability: rich description of the HDSS setting, recruitment approach, participant group, and co-creation process.
  • Participatory validity: youth co-creators’ review of themes, pathways, causal diagrams, and manuscript or dissemination materials.

Section E: Outputs, Constraints, and Reporting Decisions

E1 Planned Outputs

The qualitative analysis will produce:

  1. A cleaned thematic codebook with deductive and inductive codes.
  2. A thematic summary of youth-identified social determinants.
  3. A pathway inventory containing the 45 youth-defined causal chains and any later revisions.
  4. A pathway-to-secondary-data mapping table.
  5. A youth-informed causal overview diagram for DAG development.
  6. A post-secondary interpretation memo once quantitative findings are available.
  7. Methods text describing Photovoice, thematic analysis, pathway mapping, member checking, and participatory validation.
  8. Plain-language dissemination materials for community and stakeholder engagement.

E2 Analysis Constraints and Limitations

The analysis is designed for depth, contextual validity, and analytic meaning-making, not representativeness. The recruitment approach was non-probability based, the implemented youth group comprised 11 young adults aged 18-24, and findings should not be interpreted as prevalence estimates for all adolescents or young people in the HDSS.

The pathway inventory identifies many possible mechanisms but does not rank or quantify their relative importance. Further participatory work would be needed if the project wants youth co-creators to prioritize pathways formally.

Some locally salient domains, including culture, spirituality, housing, water insecurity, service stigma, school climate, and environmental degradation, are not directly captured in the current harmonized secondary data. These gaps should be reported as substantive findings about the limits of available data, not as failures of the qualitative work.

E3 Reporting Decisions to Track

Future reporting should track:

  • any changes to the pathway inventory after additional youth review;
  • whether pathways are prioritized, ranked, or left unweighted;
  • which photographs are approved for public use;
  • how the qualitative pathway domains are mapped to final quantitative variables;
  • how the youth-informed causal diagram is translated into formal DAGs;
  • whether the post-secondary interpretation workshops produce new themes or revisions to dissemination messages;
  • how youth co-creators review and approve final public-facing materials.
