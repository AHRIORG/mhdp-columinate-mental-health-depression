Youth Co-Creation Qualitative Analysis Plan

Qualitative analysis plan for the Photovoice-based youth co-creation component of CO-LUMINATE.

Overview

This document specifies the qualitative and participatory analysis procedures for the CO-LUMINATE youth co-creation workstream. It sits alongside, rather than inside, the Statistical Analysis Plan, which covers the quantitative analyses for Objectives 2-5.

The analysis plan is intentionally restricted to planned methods, decision rules, analytic roles, governance, and outputs. Findings from the implemented Photovoice and co-creation process are reported separately on the Objective 1 Youth Co-Creation deliverables page.

The current release covers:

  • Section A: Purpose, rationale, and study design
  • Section B: Data sources, planned activities, and analytic objectives
  • Section C: Thematic analysis, pathway mapping, and quantitative crosswalk
  • Section D: Participatory validation, ethics, reflexivity, and trustworthiness
  • Section E: Planned outputs, anticipated constraints, and versioned decisions

Section A: Purpose, Rationale, and Study Design

A1 Purpose

The purpose of this plan is to specify how the youth co-creation component will generate and analyse qualitative evidence on the social determinants of young people’s mental health in the AHRI Health and Demographic Surveillance System setting.

The qualitative work has two linked roles:

  1. To produce a youth-informed conceptual and pathway framework for understanding social determinants of youth mental health.
  2. To provide contextual grounding for later quantitative analyses, including variable interpretation, causal diagram development, pathway screening, results interpretation, and dissemination.

The analysis is not designed to create a participant-level statistical dataset or to estimate population prevalence. Its central products are a thematic account, a structured pathway inventory, and a participatory causal framework.

A2 Background and Rationale

CO-LUMINATE examines how social determinants shape adolescent and youth mental health in rural KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. The quantitative component links harmonized mental health measures to longitudinal HDSS data. Secondary data can support longitudinal modelling, but it cannot fully determine which determinants matter locally, how they are experienced, or which mechanisms young people see as linking social conditions to mental health.

Objective 1 addresses this gap through youth co-creation. Youth co-creators use Photovoice, group narrative-building, and participatory pathway mapping to identify locally meaningful determinants and mechanisms. The analysis is therefore designed to reduce distance between technical analytic decisions and the lived realities of the young people whose communities and life-course contexts are represented in the CO-LUMINATE data.

The analysis is grounded in two complementary epistemological positions. First, it uses a critical realist orientation, treating social determinants as operating through context-specific generative mechanisms. Second, it uses a social constructionist orientation, recognizing that accounts of community, risk, support, and mental health are produced through language, relationships, and power. Together, these positions support an analysis that is pathway-oriented, participatory, and attentive to local meaning.

A3 Study Design

The qualitative component is a participatory youth co-creation study using Photovoice. It is best described as deep participatory co-creation within a larger mixed-methods program, rather than full Participatory Action Research. The broader CO-LUMINATE question and quantitative design were developed before the youth co-creation component, but youth co-creators hold interpretive authority over their photographs, narratives, themes, and pathway representations.

The work has two analytic phases:

  • Pre-secondary analysis phase: develop a youth-informed inventory of social determinants, mechanisms, and causal pathways before formal interpretation of quantitative results.
  • Post-secondary analysis phase: use youth interpretation workshops after quantitative outputs are available to contextualize findings, identify what measured variables miss, and support accessible dissemination.

A4 Planned Participants and Setting

The youth co-creation work will be based in the AHRI HDSS in uMkhanyakude, KwaZulu-Natal. The target participant group is a youth co-creation or advisory group of approximately ten young people living in the HDSS area. The planned age range follows the broader CO-LUMINATE youth framing of 13-24 years, with final implementation depending on ethical approvals, safeguarding requirements, recruitment feasibility, and consent procedures.

Recruitment will aim for variation across HDSS communities where feasible. Recruitment materials should describe the role as a co-creation activity focused on youth perspectives on social determinants and mental health, rather than as diagnostic recruitment for mental health conditions.

Section B: Data Sources, Planned Activities, and Analytic Objectives

B1 Data Sources

The qualitative corpus will comprise:

  • photographs generated by youth co-creators during Photovoice activities;
  • selected and titled photographs presented in narrative-building sessions;
  • audio recordings and live notes from narrative-building discussions;
  • facilitator notes and analytic memos;
  • handwritten or typed member-checking notes in isiZulu or English;
  • the thematic codebook and coded data managed in qualitative analysis software;
  • pathway mapping sheets used to document causal chains;
  • youth-reviewed causal diagrams or pathway maps;
  • post-secondary interpretation-workshop outputs once quantitative results are available.

Photographs, captions, workshop narratives, and pathway notes will be analysed together rather than as independent data streams.

B2 Planned Co-Creation Activities

Activity Planned analytic role
Recruitment and onboarding Establish the youth co-creation group, confirm consent, and explain roles, benefits, risks, and safeguarding procedures
Inception workshop Build shared understanding of adolescent mental health, social determinants, Photovoice ethics, and the purpose of co-creation
Photovoice walkabout or photo-generation activity Generate visual data on physical, social, and symbolic determinants of youth mental health
Narrative-building workshops Elicit photograph titles, meanings, local explanations, and group reflections
Thematic analysis Develop deductive and inductive codes, candidate themes, and thematic summaries
Pathway mapping Translate themes and narratives into youth-defined causal chains and pathway diagrams
Member checking Review themes, interpretations, pathways, and public-use materials with youth co-creators
Post-secondary interpretation workshops Interpret quantitative findings in relation to youth-defined pathways and dissemination needs

B3 Analytic Objectives

The qualitative analysis has five objectives:

  1. Identify social determinants of mental health that youth co-creators consider locally salient.
  2. Characterize how these determinants are understood to operate through risk and protective mechanisms.
  3. Distinguish root causes, intervening mechanisms, and mental health outcomes in youth-defined causal accounts.
  4. Translate qualitative pathway accounts into a structured pathway inventory that can inform quantitative variable selection, DAG development, and interpretation.
  5. Document good practices for co-creating an analytic framework with young people in an under-resourced rural setting.

B4 Research Questions

The analysis will answer the following questions:

  1. Which social determinants do youth co-creators identify as shaping young people’s mental health in the AHRI HDSS setting?
  2. What risk and protective mechanisms do youth co-creators describe between these determinants and mental health outcomes?
  3. Which determinants and mechanisms can be translated into causal pathway representations?
  4. Which youth-defined pathway domains are captured, partially captured, or absent in the CO-LUMINATE secondary data platform?
  5. How should youth co-creator interpretations inform causal diagrams, pathway-screening decisions, results interpretation, and dissemination?

Section C: Thematic Analysis, Pathway Mapping, and Quantitative Crosswalk

C1 Analytic Orientation

The analysis will use a hybrid inductive-deductive thematic approach. Deductive coding will be guided by established social determinants of health and social determinants of mental health frameworks. These frameworks will operate as sensitizing concepts rather than as a fixed coding structure.

The initial deductive framework includes:

  • socioeconomic position, poverty, and economic inequality;
  • education, employment, and working conditions;
  • housing and living conditions;
  • early childhood development;
  • social support and social capital;
  • discrimination and social exclusion;
  • physical environment;
  • access to basic services;
  • violence, crime, and safety;
  • governance and policy.

Inductive codes will be added when youth co-creators describe locally important meanings or pathways that are not adequately captured by the deductive framework. These inductive codes may become themes if they are coherent, well supported, and validated through member checking.

Themes will be treated as interpretive constructs that capture shared patterns of meaning, not as simple frequency counts. Frequency of discussion may help describe salience, but it will not be the sole basis for importance. A determinant may be analytically important because it clarifies a mechanism, reveals a local meaning, or exposes a gap in the secondary data even if it appears less often.

C2 Thematic Analysis Procedure

The thematic analysis will follow six phases adapted from reflexive thematic analysis:

  1. Familiarisation: review photographs, titles, transcripts, notes, and workshop discussions in their full context.
  2. Initial coding: code photo and narrative content using both the deductive social determinants framework and inductively generated codes.
  3. Theme development: group related codes into candidate themes that represent shared meaning across images and narratives.
  4. Theme review: compare candidate themes against the full corpus, checking whether each theme is coherent, distinct, and supported.
  5. Theme definition and naming: refine theme names, analytic scope, and boundaries, prioritizing language that preserves youth co-creators’ intended meanings.
  6. Analytic write-up: produce a thematic narrative with exemplar photographs, captions, and selected short excerpts where appropriate and ethically permitted.

C3 Pathway Mapping Procedure

After thematic coding, a second analytic pass will identify causal chains linking determinants to mental health outcomes. Each pathway will be entered into a structured pathway matrix with the following fields:

Field Description
Pathway ID Stable identifier for each youth-defined pathway
Thematic domain Primary determinant domain
Root cause or exposure Social, structural, environmental, or relational condition described by youth co-creators
Intervening mechanism(s) Psychological, social, behavioural, service, or material process linking exposure to mental health
Direction Risk, protective, or mixed
Outcome Poor mental health, positive mental health, or both
Evidence source Photograph, caption, narrative workshop, member-checking note, or pathway sheet
Secondary-data capture Directly captured, partially captured, not directly captured, or interpretive context only
Quantitative implication Relevance for variable selection, DAG development, interpretation, or dissemination

The pathway inventory will not be ranked or weighted unless a later participatory exercise explicitly asks youth co-creators to assign importance, strength, or priority. Until then, pathways will be reported as a structured qualitative inventory rather than as a quantitative hierarchy.

Any overview causal diagram should classify concepts as:

  • root causes or upstream determinants;
  • intervening causes or mechanisms;
  • risk pathways;
  • protective pathways;
  • poor mental health outcomes;
  • positive mental health outcomes.

The diagram will be used as a bridge into formal DAG development for the quantitative component. It should not be treated as a final statistical DAG without additional causal-structure review by the quantitative team.

C4 Crosswalk to Quantitative Analysis

The qualitative analysis contributes to CO-LUMINATE through a pathway inventory rather than a participant-level analytic dataset. Its main function is to make explicit which youth-defined mechanisms are measured, partially measured, or absent in the secondary data.

The pathway-to-quantitative crosswalk will use the following structure:

Crosswalk field Planned decision rule
Youth-informed pathway domain Use the member-checked theme or pathway-domain label
Candidate quantitative variable Identify available harmonized variable(s), if any
Capture classification Classify as directly captured, partially captured, not directly captured, or interpretive context only
Interpretation note Describe what the variable captures and what it misses
Quantitative use Specify whether the domain informs variable selection, DAG development, interpretation, dissemination, or future measurement

This crosswalk should be updated whenever the harmonized quantitative data platform changes.

Section D: Participatory Validation, Ethics, Reflexivity, and Trustworthiness

D1 Member Checking and Participatory Validation

Member checking is a core analytic step rather than an optional dissemination activity. Youth co-creators will review thematic summaries, pathway interpretations, and causal diagrams to confirm that the analysis preserves intended meanings and does not impose external interpretations that contradict their accounts.

Member-checking workshops should document:

  • areas of agreement with draft themes and pathways;
  • changes requested by youth co-creators;
  • disagreements or divergent interpretations;
  • themes or pathways that youth co-creators consider missing;
  • wording changes needed to preserve local meaning;
  • decisions about which photographs or excerpts may be used publicly;
  • youth co-creators’ interpretation of the final overview diagram.

Where youth co-creators disagree with the research team’s interpretation, the youth interpretation should be prioritized for photographs and narratives generated by them. Researcher analytic memos may document alternative interpretations, but should not override youth co-creators’ stated meanings.

D2 Post-Secondary Interpretation Phase

After quantitative analyses are available, youth co-creators should be invited to structured interpretation workshops. These workshops will focus on aggregate findings only and will not expose individual-level participant data.

The interpretation workshops should ask:

  1. Which quantitative findings feel consistent with youth co-creators’ lived understanding of the community?
  2. Which findings feel surprising, incomplete, or potentially misleading without context?
  3. Which unmeasured pathways from the qualitative inventory help explain the quantitative results?
  4. Which messages should be prioritized for young people, families, community stakeholders, services, and policy actors?
  5. Which photographs, diagrams, or plain-language materials best support dissemination?

The output of this phase should be a structured interpretation memo linking quantitative findings to youth co-creator reflections. This memo can support the website, manuscripts, presentations, infographics, and the Photovoice exhibition or townhall dissemination event.

D3 Ethics, Safeguarding, and Data Protection

The co-creation component will follow the relevant ethics approvals, informed-consent requirements, youth safeguarding procedures, and data protection requirements for CO-LUMINATE and AHRI.

The Photovoice process should include specific ethical protections:

  • avoid identifiable faces in photographs unless appropriate consent is in place;
  • obtain consent before photographing other people;
  • avoid public disclosure of sensitive or identifying information;
  • discuss benefits, risks, roles, and safeguards during onboarding;
  • conduct activities in ways that respond to youth safeguarding needs;
  • use activity formats and transport arrangements that reduce physical safety risks;
  • conduct interpretation and translation checks across isiZulu and English to preserve meaning.

Any public-facing use of photographs, captions, or excerpts should be reviewed against consent, identifiability, sensitivity, and youth co-creator preferences before release.

D4 Reflexivity and Power

The analysis recognizes power differences between academic researchers and youth co-creators. Academic researchers contribute methodological, analytic, and institutional expertise. Youth co-creators contribute proximal knowledge of community life, social conditions, and the meanings attached to photographs and narratives.

To reduce extractive interpretation, the analysis will use:

  • explicit reflexive memos about facilitator assumptions and framing effects;
  • transparent documentation of deductive frameworks used by the research team;
  • independent coding followed by cross-checking and discussion;
  • member checking with youth co-creators;
  • co-authorship and acknowledgement practices where appropriate;
  • youth approval of final themes, pathways, and selected public-facing materials.

D5 Trustworthiness

Trustworthiness will be supported through:

  • Credibility: member checking, interpretation workshops, use of photographs and narratives together, and attention to disconfirming or divergent accounts.
  • Dependability: documentation of coding decisions, pathway-mapping rules, workshop stages, and changes made after member checking.
  • Confirmability: reflexive memos, preservation of an audit trail, and transparent separation between youth interpretation and researcher synthesis.
  • Transferability: rich description of the HDSS setting, recruitment approach, participant group, and co-creation process.
  • Participatory validity: youth co-creators’ review of themes, pathways, causal diagrams, and manuscript or dissemination materials.

Section E: Planned Outputs, Anticipated Constraints, and Versioned Decisions

E1 Planned Outputs

The qualitative analysis will produce:

  1. A cleaned thematic codebook with deductive and inductive codes.
  2. A thematic summary of youth-identified social determinants.
  3. A pathway inventory of youth-defined causal chains.
  4. A pathway-to-secondary-data mapping table.
  5. A youth-informed causal overview diagram for DAG development.
  6. A post-secondary interpretation memo once quantitative findings are available.
  7. A website-ready qualitative analysis-plan page.
  8. A separate website-ready results report.
  9. Plain-language dissemination materials for community and stakeholder engagement.

E2 Anticipated Constraints

The analysis is designed for depth, contextual validity, and analytic meaning-making, not representativeness. Recruitment is expected to be non-probability based, and findings should not be interpreted as prevalence estimates for all adolescents or young people in the HDSS.

The pathway inventory may identify many possible mechanisms but will not quantify their relative importance unless a participatory prioritization exercise is added.

Some youth-defined domains may not be directly captured in the harmonized secondary data. These gaps should be reported as substantive findings about the limits of available data and as priorities for future measurement.

E3 Versioned Decisions to Track

Future revisions should track:

  • any change in eligibility, recruitment, or youth co-creator age range;
  • any changes to the pathway inventory after youth review;
  • whether pathways are prioritized, ranked, or left unweighted;
  • which photographs are approved for public use;
  • how qualitative pathway domains are mapped to final quantitative variables;
  • how the youth-informed causal diagram is translated into formal DAGs;
  • whether post-secondary interpretation workshops produce new themes or revisions to dissemination messages;
  • how youth co-creators review and approve final public-facing materials.
