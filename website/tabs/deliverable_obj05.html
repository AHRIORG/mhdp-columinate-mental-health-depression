Objective 1: Youth Co-Creation

Objective-level results page for the Photovoice-based youth co-creation workstream.

Objective

Co-create a shared understanding of how social determinants shape young people’s mental health and translate youth-defined meanings into a pathway framework that can inform later CO-LUMINATE analyses.

Summary

The Objective 1 co-creation work used Photovoice to involve youth co-creators in identifying social determinants of mental health in the AHRI HDSS setting and mapping how those determinants may shape poor and positive mental health. The work produced a youth-informed thematic framework, a set of causal pathways, and an overview causal diagram that can inform later quantitative DAG development and interpretation.

The main result is not a statistical dataset. It is a contextual pathway inventory that clarifies how young people understand material deprivation, service failure, violence, social support, education, employment, housing, environmental conditions, and spiritual or cultural resources as part of the mental-health pathway ecology.

The corresponding qualitative analysis plan is available under Technical Documentation: Youth Co-Creation Qualitative Analysis Plan.

Implemented Design and Sample

The study was conducted in the AHRI HDSS in the Mtubatuba Local Municipality within the Hlabisa Health Sub-District, uMkhanyakude, KwaZulu-Natal. Activities were hosted at AHRI’s Somkhele campus.

The implemented co-creation group included 11 youth co-creators aged 18-24 years living in the AHRI HDSS area. Recruitment took place from 5 September 2025 to 12 September 2025 using word of mouth, snowball sampling, WhatsApp adverts, and recruitment across diverse geographic clusters within the HDSS.

The group included six males and five females. Ten out of 11 youth co-creators had finished school, three were parents, and none were employed at the time the research took place.

The original proposal anticipated approximately ten youth advisory group members aged 13-24. The implemented Photovoice component focused on young adults aged 18-24, and this distinction is retained in public-facing reporting.

Analytic Products

The co-creation process produced:

  • a youth-informed thematic structure for social determinants of mental health;
  • a supplementary inventory of 45 youth-defined causal pathways;
  • a synthesis table of main risk and protective pathways;
  • an overview causal diagram linking social determinants, intervening mechanisms, and mental health outcomes;
  • a crosswalk between youth-informed pathway domains and currently measured secondary-data domains.

No photographs, captions, or direct quotations are published on this page. Any future public use of photographs, captions, or excerpts should be reviewed against consent, identifiability, sensitivity, and youth co-creator preferences before release.

Thematic Results

The analysis identified nine substantive thematic domains. These domains reflect social determinants and pathway mechanisms discussed by youth co-creators through photographs, captions, narratives, and member-checking reflections.

Theme Result summary
Access to basic services Service and infrastructure gaps, including water, electricity, healthcare access, policing, transport, and public infrastructure, were described as material stressors and symbols of institutional neglect.
Violence, crime, and safety Young people described interpersonal violence, domestic violence, substance-related insecurity, unsafe community spaces, road hazards, abandoned buildings, and the emotional cost of chronic vigilance.
Socioeconomic position, poverty, and economic inequality Poverty, unemployment, food stress, livestock loss, grant reliance, and household financial pressure were repeatedly linked to stress, conflict, shame, and loss of hope.
Social support, social capital, exclusion, and discrimination Family, community, churches, sports groups, and social networks were described as sources of support, but exclusion, stigma, family pressure, and weak youth voice were also important stressors.
Physical environment Mining, pollution, dumping, flooding, water scarcity, damaged land, and climate-related crop loss were linked to fear, grief, illness, and financial strain, while land and agriculture also offered resilience.
Culture, religion, and spirituality Churches, prayer, rituals, ancestral connection, and pastoral support were described as protective, while spiritual conflict, stigma, and fear of harmful practices created strain.
Education, employment, and working conditions School safety, corporal punishment, truancy, weak monitoring, unemployment, mining employment, and protracted job searches shaped agency, self-esteem, and future orientation.
Housing and living conditions Poor housing, weak sanitation, lack of running water, unsafe bridges, road conditions, and household discomfort were linked to shame, bullying, worry, and demoralisation.
Early childhood development Local creches and early learning spaces were seen as protective resources that support school readiness, safety, food access, and later opportunity.

The most frequently discussed themes in the manuscript were access to basic services and violence, crime, and safety. Social support and exclusion, socioeconomic position and poverty, and the physical environment were also prominent.

Interpretation for CO-LUMINATE

The youth co-creation results strengthen the wider CO-LUMINATE analysis in four ways.

First, they support the local relevance of measured domains such as food insecurity, violence exposure, alcohol use, sexual behaviour, and grant conditions.

Second, they show that measured variables are only a constrained subset of the broader pathway ecology described by young people.

Third, they identify domains that should be carried into interpretation even when they cannot be directly modelled, including housing, water insecurity, service stigma, social connectedness, school climate, spiritual and cultural resources, and environmental degradation.

Fourth, they provide youth-informed causal language that can inform DAG development, manuscript framing, public-facing deliverables, and stakeholder dissemination.

Status

  • Objective 1 has produced a youth-informed thematic structure, a 45-pathway inventory, and a pathway-to-secondary-data crosswalk.
  • The pre-secondary co-creation results now serve as contextual input to pathway screening, causal diagram development, and interpretation across the wider project.
  • Post-secondary interpretation workshops remain planned after the main quantitative outputs are available.
